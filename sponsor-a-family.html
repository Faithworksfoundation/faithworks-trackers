Families stay private. Always.

Sponsoring is generous — it should never cost a family their dignity. So Faithworks stays in the middle from start to finish, and a family's private information never changes hands.

  • You see: ages, clothing & shoe sizes, and the wish list — everyone listed as Mom, Dad, Child One, Child Two, never by name.
  • You never see: names, addresses, phone numbers, or emails.
  • Families never get your contact info either — just their gifts, and the knowledge that a neighbor cared.

Good questions

How much does it cost?

Less than you might think — one person runs about $80: two things they need, plus one wish. A whole family costs more and it varies a lot, roughly $333 to $1,040 at regular prices depending on their wishes, averaging around $400 — every profile shows its own estimate, so you'll know the real number before you choose. And if a full family or even a full person isn't in the cards, a partial gift still counts — donate any amount or shop part of a list, and we'll combine gifts to complete it.

When are gifts due?

One week before the family pickup celebration. The celebration date will be announced soon, and we'll email every sponsor the exact due date and drop-off details as soon as it's set.

Can a group sponsor a family?

Absolutely — church groups, workplaces, and extended families do this together all the time. One person signs up as the contact, and the group splits the list or the cost however you like.

Is my gift tax-deductible?

Faithworks Foundation Inc. is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit, so donations are tax-deductible. Donate through PayPal and you'll get a receipt automatically; if you shop the list yourself, email us and we'll send a donation acknowledgment for the gifts you dropped off.

What if the family I picked is already sponsored?

We'll let you know right away and offer you the next family waiting — there are more families than sponsors right now, so nobody goes unmatched.

Can I meet the family?

To protect their privacy, sponsors and families don't meet or exchange contact information. What you can do is include a short unsigned note with the gifts — families tell us those notes mean the world.
